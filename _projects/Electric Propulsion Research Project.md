# Electrospray Electric Propulsion Research

**Cornell ASTRA Lab | Summer 2026**  
Undergraduate Researcher

During Summer 2026, I conducted research in Cornell’s ASTRA Lab focused on **electrospray electric propulsion and plume-surface interactions with spacecraft structural materials**.

My work investigated how electrospray thruster plumes interact with nearby spacecraft surfaces, with a focus on **erosion, deposition, and long-term material durability**. The goal of this research was to better understand how plume-surface interactions can affect spacecraft hardware and propulsion system lifetime.

## My Work

I contributed to both the experimental setup and analysis of the research. My work included:

- Designing and manufacturing electromechanical components for thruster operation and vacuum testing
- Conducting vacuum chamber testing with an electrospray propulsion system
- Testing spacecraft structural materials including aluminum and tungsten
- Measuring plume-induced erosion and deposition using profilometry
- Characterizing exposed surfaces using scanning electron microscopy (SEM)
- Developing MATLAB tools for experimental data analysis
- Supporting heater and thruster hardware development
- Analyzing how plume effects vary spatially relative to the thruster plume

## Experimental Approach

Material samples were exposed to an electrospray plume under vacuum conditions and then analyzed using several surface characterization techniques.

The experimental setup was operated inside a vacuum chamber, where the electrospray thruster and material samples could be tested under low-pressure conditions.

![Vacuum chamber experimental setup]({{ '/assets/vaccuum_chamber.jpeg' | relative_url }})

*Vacuum chamber setup used for electrospray plume-surface interaction testing.*

**Profilometry** was used to quantify changes in surface height and estimate net volumetric erosion or deposition.

**Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)** was used to examine changes in surface morphology at higher resolution.

These measurements were used to compare how different spacecraft materials respond to electrospray plume exposure and to better understand potential material degradation mechanisms.
